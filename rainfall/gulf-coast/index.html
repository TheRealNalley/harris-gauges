How to read these numbers

USGS rain gauges report the rain that fell in each 15-minute interval (5 minutes at a few); every figure here adds those up. "Last 24 hours" runs to each gauge's latest report; gauges that haven't reported in 3 hours show no current total. A day is the calendar day in the gauge's own time zone, so a storm that runs past midnight counts toward two days. The Houston-area county gauges on Houston-area rainfall report rolling 24-hour totals instead, so the two pages' figures aren't ranked together.

The readings are provisional and are published as USGS publishes them. Rain gauges can clog, fail or under-read in wind; a single interval reporting more than 3 inches is treated as a sensor fault and left out of every total. Nothing here is verified independently, and a rainfall total says nothing about flooding at any street or property. USGS runs few rain gauges east of the Mississippi River: 9 in the coastal counties of Mississippi, Alabama and Florida. How this site works
