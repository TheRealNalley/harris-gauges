Lake Texana is a water-supply lake, run by the Lavaca-Navidad River Authority. It is normally held near a steady level, rising after heavy rain and falling in dry spells.

Elevation is the height of the lake surface above NAVD88 (a fixed survey reference), not a water depth. USGS publishes no storage figure for this lake.

Over the last 12 months it has been as low as 49.6% (April 10, 2026) and as full as 100.0% (May 30, 2026).

Percent full is the Texas Water Development Board's measure: the water in the lake's conservation pool (the part kept for water supply, above any dead pool that can't be drawn) as a share of what that pool holds when full. It stops at 100%: water above the conservation pool is in flood storage. The figures are daily estimates worked out from the lake's latest survey, so they can differ a little from USGS's storage figure. TWDB's page for Lake Texana has its full record.
