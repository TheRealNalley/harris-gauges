Lake Corpus Christi is a water-supply lake, run by the City of Corpus Christi. It is normally held near a steady level, rising after heavy rain and falling in dry spells.

Elevation is the height of the lake surface above NGVD29 (a fixed survey reference), not a water depth. Storage is USGS's estimate of the water held, in acre-feet (one acre-foot covers an acre a foot deep).

No National Weather Service alert covering Jim Wells County was active when this page was generated (Nov 14, 2026 at 3:20 AM CST). Alerts are issued and expire at any time; the current list is at alerts.weather.gov.

Over the last 12 months it has been as low as 8.3% (April 17, 2026) and as full as 92.0% (August 15, 2026).

Percent full is the Texas Water Development Board's measure: the water in the lake's conservation pool (the part kept for water supply, above any dead pool that can't be drawn) as a share of what that pool holds when full. It stops at 100%: water above the conservation pool is in flood storage. The figures are daily estimates worked out from the lake's latest survey, so they can differ a little from USGS's storage figure. TWDB's page for Lake Corpus Christi has its full record.
