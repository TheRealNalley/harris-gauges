How to read these numbers

None of the numbers is a depth. Lakefront Airport (Army Corps) and Crossover 4 (USGS) report the water surface elevation above NAVD88, a fixed survey reference. Mandeville (USGS) reports gage height above the gauge's own zero, which USGS puts 0.14 ft below NAVD88. New Canal (NOAA) reports the level relative to mean higher high water (MHHW), the average height of the higher of each day's two high tides there, so 0 ft at New Canal is an ordinary high tide rather than a survey elevation.

Wind can push water toward one shore and away from the other, so the north and south shore gauges don't always move together. The NWS publishes coastal flood thresholds for New Canal; it publishes no flood stages for the other three.

Highest levels on record

  • New Canal Station: 5.97 ft above MHHW, August 30, 2012 (NOAA, highest observed).
  • L Pontchartrain at Crossover 4 near Mandeville: 6.14 ft (October 28, 1985), the highest NWS historic crest.
  • L Pontchartrain at Crossover 4 near Mandeville: 6.73 ft on August 28, 2021, the highest USGS annual peak since 2016.

Each on its own gauge's reference, as published by the agency; the records cover different years at each gauge.
