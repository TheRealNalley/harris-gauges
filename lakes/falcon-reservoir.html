Falcon Reservoir is an international reservoir on the Rio Grande, shared by the United States and Mexico and run by the two countries' sections of the International Boundary and Water Commission, for water conservation, flood control and hydropower. Its level rises after heavy rain in the Rio Grande basin upstream and falls as water is released and used downstream.

Elevation is the height of the lake surface above NGVD29 (a fixed survey reference), not a water depth.

No National Weather Service alert covering Starr and Zapata counties was active when this page was generated (Nov 14, 2026 at 1:35 AM CST). Alerts are issued and expire at any time; the current list is at alerts.weather.gov.

Percent full is the Texas Water Development Board's measure: the water in the lake's conservation pool (the part kept for water supply, above any dead pool that can't be drawn) as a share of what that pool holds when full. It stops at 100%: water above the conservation pool is in flood storage. The figures are daily estimates worked out from the lake's latest survey. For Falcon, every TWDB figure is Texas's share: by treaty, 58.6% of the lake's conservation capacity, measured against the share of the water IBWC assigns to Texas every two weeks. TWDB's page for Falcon Reservoir has its full record.
