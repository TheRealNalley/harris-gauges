Sam Rayburn Reservoir is a multipurpose reservoir, run by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers: flood control, hydropower and water supply. It is held near a normal level, with room above it to store floodwater, so it rises after heavy rain upstream and is drawn down again afterwards.

Elevation is the height of the lake surface above NGVD29 (a fixed survey reference), not a water depth. USGS publishes no storage figure for this lake.

Over the last 12 months it has been as low as 59.8% (November 21, 2026) and as full as 73.0% (July 1, 2026).

Percent full is the Texas Water Development Board's measure: the water in the lake's conservation pool (the part kept for water supply, above any dead pool that can't be drawn) as a share of what that pool holds when full. It stops at 100%: water above the conservation pool is in flood storage. The figures are daily estimates worked out from the lake's latest survey, so they can differ a little from USGS's storage figure. TWDB's page for Sam Rayburn Reservoir has its full record.
