Toledo Bend Reservoir is a water-supply lake, run by the Sabine River Authorities of Texas and Louisiana. It is normally held near a steady level, rising after heavy rain and falling in dry spells.

Elevation is the height of the lake surface above NGVD29 (a fixed survey reference), not a water depth. Storage is USGS's estimate of the water held, in acre-feet (one acre-foot covers an acre a foot deep).

No National Weather Service alert covering Newton County was active when this page was generated (Nov 21, 2026 at 10:50 AM CST). Alerts are issued and expire at any time; the current list is at alerts.weather.gov.

Over the last 12 months it has been as low as 83.4% (November 24, 2025) and as full as 96.1% (June 23, 2026).

Percent full is the Texas Water Development Board's measure: the water in the lake's conservation pool (the part kept for water supply, above any dead pool that can't be drawn) as a share of what that pool holds when full. It stops at 100%: water above the conservation pool is in flood storage. The figures are daily estimates worked out from the lake's latest survey, so they can differ a little from USGS's storage figure. For Toledo Bend, TWDB's acre-feet are Texas's share, half the lake; the percentage is the same for the whole lake. TWDB's page for Toledo Bend Reservoir has its full record.
