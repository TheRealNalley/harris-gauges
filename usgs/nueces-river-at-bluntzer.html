How to read this gauge

Gage height is the height of the water surface above this gauge's own zero point, which sits at -0.31 ft NAVD88. It is not a water depth and not an elevation: add -0.31 ft to get the water surface elevation.

The flood stages are the National Weather Service's, on the same scale as the gage height; they are the NWS's references for this location, not estimates made here.

Specific conductance is how readily the water carries an electric current, adjusted to 25 °C, in microsiemens per centimeter; it rises with dissolved salts. Fresh river water is usually a few hundred µS/cm and seawater about 50,000. Dissolved oxygen is the oxygen held in the water, in milligrams per liter; cool water holds more than warm water, and it rises and falls over the day as plants and algae grow. pH is how acidic or basic the water is, on a scale of 0 to 14 where 7 is neutral. Turbidity is how cloudy the water is, in formazin nephelometric units (FNU); it usually rises when runoff carries sediment into the water. Provisional readings from USGS, which can be revised.
